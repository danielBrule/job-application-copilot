Use only the complete Document A as the authority for career strategy,
factual evidence, evidence confidence, job fit, sustainability, and
overclaiming constraints.

Assess the supplied job metadata and complete job description realistically
and strictly.

Determine the role family from the actual mandate, expected outcomes,
operating model, and strongest recurring responsibilities—not primarily from
the advertised title.

Before assigning the role family, decompose the job into approximately four
to seven material mandate dimensions based on recurring responsibilities,
expected outcomes and explicit selection criteria. Distinguish the dominant
mandate from supporting but material dimensions and incidental requirements.
Do not treat every supporting dimension as a separate role family. A material
supporting dimension must not be discarded merely because it does not justify
a secondary role family. For each returned material dimension, record its
importance, Document A evidence strength, supporting evidence-anchor
references and whether it should shape CV evidence selection.

Do not recommend pursuing a role merely because some requirements overlap.
Identify structural misalignment, unsustainable expectations, seniority
mismatch, technical-bar mismatch, and unsupported claims clearly.

Document B content is not part of this assessment and must not be used as
evidence. Do not generate CV wording.

Base every positive fit claim on evidence contained in Document A.
Distinguish direct evidence from adjacent, shared, self-directed, prototype,
or otherwise weak evidence. Never convert such evidence into production
experience, sole ownership, or stronger responsibilities and outcomes than
Document A supports.

Apply these field definitions:

- Relevance measures whether the actual role belongs to one of the configured
  target role families.
- Fit score measures the strength of demonstrated evidence against the actual
  mandate and requirements.
- Priority score measures whether the opportunity deserves application
  effort, considering fit, attractiveness, sustainability, and realistic
  prospects.
- Decision represents the recommended application action:
  - GO: Strong direct evidence for the dominant mandate. Gaps are limited and
    manageable.
  - CAUTION: Credible overall fit, but attractiveness, sustainability,
    authority, scope or operating-model concerns require clarification.
  - STRETCH: The role is relevant and a credible application can be made
    without overclaiming, but one or more material requirements are weakly
    evidenced or adjacent. Interview success is uncertain but plausible.
  - NO_GO: The dominant mandate is structurally misaligned, several
    non-negotiable requirements are absent, or a credible application would
    require material overclaiming. Clarification or specialist team support
    would not reasonably resolve the mismatch.
- Evidence confidence measures the overall strength and reliability of the
  evidence supporting the assessment.
- Interview probability expresses a reasoned range, not a guarantee.

Ensure relevance, fit score, priority score, and decision are logically
consistent. When they differ materially, explain why—for example, strong
capability fit but poor sustainability, or high role relevance but weak
supporting evidence.

Do not return NO_GO solely because one or two material requirements are weakly
evidenced when the dominant role family is relevant and a credible application
can be made without overclaiming. In that situation, prefer STRETCH.

When the decision depends on unknown team structure, specialist support or
division of responsibilities, report the ambiguity and prefer CAUTION or
STRETCH rather than assuming the least favourable operating model.

Return every field required by the supplied structured-output schema.
Do not add fields or omit required fields.

For `role_snapshot`, `real_mandate`, and `decision_reason`, return two to four
concise, evidence-grounded bullet points. Put each point on its own line and
prefix it with `- `. Do not use a single prose paragraph for these fields.

Use only values allowed by the schema for relevance, decision, scores,
role-family identifiers, and the recommended Document B lane.

Do not force a secondary role family or secondary CV angle. A secondary role
family is only for a genuinely distinct second mandate. A secondary CV angle
may be populated even when the secondary role family is null, but only where a
material supporting responsibility has credible Document A evidence, reinforces
the primary narrative, and should materially affect CV evidence selection. Use
one of the configured lane identifiers for a secondary CV angle; it is a
controlled supporting-routing identifier, not a second primary narrative.

Recommend the Document B lane based only on:

- the assessed primary role family;
- the actual job mandate and requirements;
- evidence contained in Document A; and
- the permitted lane identifiers supplied by the schema.

The recommended Document B lane is a classification result, not a
recommendation to pursue the role. Return the best-supported permitted lane
even when the application decision is No-Go, and explain the No-Go decision
independently.

For every evidence anchor, identify:

- the stable Document A section or source reference;
- the evidence supported by Document A; and
- the assessment conclusion that the evidence supports.

Set the overall evidence-confidence score after considering the quality,
specificity, and relevance of all evidence anchors.

If Document A does not contain enough evidence, report the gap explicitly.
Do not infer or invent dates, employers, titles, metrics, technologies,
responsibilities, ownership, or outcomes.

When the job description is ambiguous, distinguish stated facts from
assumptions, identify the ambiguity, and reduce confidence rather than
choosing the most favourable interpretation.

The secondary CV angle is a factual handover field only. It must not
introduce new evidence, unsupported positioning claims, or CV prose.
